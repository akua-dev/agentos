## Give First Mate a boundary, not a graph

Use the real outcome from the previous chapter. State what should become true, the evidence that
will prove it, the repository or operational boundary and the authority that remains yours. Leave
the internal Agent choreography open.

Keep that boundary exact as the Fleet learns more. Do not prescribe worker count, implementation
steps or a merge decision that remains outside the accepted authority.

You supplied the acceptance boundary and excluded authority. You did not decide in advance how
many workers exist, which implementation path will be necessary or which evidence will reveal the
cause.

## Watch current reality shape the work

First Mate reads the project instructions, tracker and current delivery posture before it accepts
or routes work. The evidence may make one ship Assignment immediately honest. It may instead show
that the organization needs one bounded fact, one human choice or another outcome first.

The original intent should remain recognizable while the route changes. AgentOS is preserving an
outcome, not executing a frozen graph.

## Distinguish four honest outcomes

Look for the smallest truthful next state:

1. **Assignment:** the work is actionable and one Agent owns returning the accepted evidence.
2. **Scout:** one bounded uncertainty needs investigation before ship work is honest.
3. **Captain decision:** a consequential choice exceeds the delegated authority and comes back to
   you with its complete options.
4. **Dependency:** another distinct outcome must land before this one can proceed.

A Scout is not a second implementation worker racing the owner. A dependency is not a vague
“blocked” label. Each state says what evidence or decision can change it.

## Observed in a real Fleet

The snapshots below are faithfully shortened from records in one authorized Fleet observed on
31 July 2026. Identifying details were replaced, while the ownership, authority boundaries and
measured results were preserved. They are separate observed outcomes, not a composite story.

### Turn an ambitious strategy into honest evidence

One Captain outcome asked the Fleet to chart an evidence-backed long-range company strategy. The
resulting graph held **15 durable Tasks**: 2 completed, 2 active, 3 blocked and 8 waiting on
dependencies. First Mate chose the smallest honest initial split: one Scout for the private
company baseline and one for the public market landscape.

The public Scout returned a 43,638-character report grounded in **57 public sources**. It ranked
candidate openings, attached confidence to each and named evidence that would falsify them. It
also refused to make the final company-specific choice: customer, pipeline, product-usage, cost,
channel and capability evidence remained outside its authorized evidence boundary. Instead of
hiding that gap behind a confident recommendation, the Fleet made the missing inputs durable.

### Repair, prove and roll out a production path

Two linked Tasks kept implementation proof separate from production authority:

1. **AI Gateway repair Task.** The owner added bounded, privacy-safe stream-failure telemetry and prevented
   cancellation or cleanup from masking the provider outcome. An authenticated long-stream test
   then reproduced 384 SSE events and 798,994 bytes byte for byte.
2. **Separately authorized release/rollout Task.** A separate owner, with its own Captain authorization,
   published an immutable release and replaced only the Gateway, preserving its durable storage,
   routing state and active Agent identities. A corrected production request completed with 2,480,890
   bytes across 7,003 chunks and **8,721 SSE events**.
   One persistent Mate then completed a canary in its existing native session. The
   broader rollout remained separately gated.

### Upgrade persistent workers without erasing the company

Another outcome upgraded **five persistent Mates** serially. Each replacement retained its Agent
identity, storage, native session, active Tasks and Assignments, private memory and child-workload
boundary. The operator replaced one Mate at a time and would stop at the first unverified
boundary. All five reached the reviewed release and reported evidence upward; credentials,
charters, ownership and unrelated external systems stayed untouched.

> **The useful non-event.** One accepted outcome asked for an approved CLI in the default Mate
> image. Inspection proved that an equivalent reviewed change was already in the published
> release, so coordination closed with no implementation and **no competing pull request**. Live
> rollout remained a separate Captain-gated outcome.

Together, these outcomes show the company loop: decide where to go, repair and ship the product,
operate persistent workers safely and avoid work that current evidence has made unnecessary.

## Check what became durable

A Task can preserve deliberate backlog, but a Task without an Assignment is not accepted
asynchronous work. Once execution is accepted, exactly one active Assignment names the accountable
owner.

New evidence may justify another Task, but only for a genuinely distinct outcome the organization
should preserve. Routine tool calls, implementation steps and raw reasoning stay in their native
session instead of becoming a shadow planning tree.

Inspect the accepted Task, its active Assignment, any Scout, decision or dependency, and the native
artifact that will prove delivery. Activity alone is not evidence.

## Know what good looks like

You can still identify the original outcome and authority boundary. Accepted work has one owner.
Uncertainty is explicit rather than hidden behind confident status. Every claim points to the
system that can prove it, and merge still belongs to you.

That is progressive planning: current evidence can change the route without losing intent,
responsibility or human control. Next, [watch and steer the
work](/learn/01-first-outcome/watch-and-steer) when a new fact makes an assumption stale.
