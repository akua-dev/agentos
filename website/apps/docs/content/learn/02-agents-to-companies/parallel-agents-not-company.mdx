## Throughput is not accountability

Launching several Agent sessions can increase throughput. A router can send prompts to specialists
and an orchestrator can run them in parallel. Neither answers who accepted the outcome, who owns it
now, what authority each worker has or what happens after interruption.

```text
five sessions noticing work ≠ one organization owning work
```

A company needs durable identity, stable intent, active ownership, hierarchy, authority, attention
and recovery. Those properties must outlive the sessions realizing them.

## Three useful sessions, no owner

A customer reports that checkout accepts an expired address. Three sessions see the support
message:

- one proposes a frontend fix;
- one inspects the API;
- one waits for more detail.

All three may help. Nobody can answer: “Who owns returning a verified outcome to the customer
workflow?” If the sessions close, the issue may be forgotten or investigated again tomorrow.

A manager model summarizing transcripts still does not create durable ownership. The next step is
to accept the external intent as one stable outcome.
