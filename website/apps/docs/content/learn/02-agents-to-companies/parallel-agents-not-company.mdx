## The mental model

Launching several Agent sessions can increase throughput. A router can send prompts to specialists
and an orchestrator can run them in parallel. Neither fact answers who accepted an outcome, who is
accountable now, what authority each worker has or what happens after interruption.

Throughput is not accountability.

```text
five sessions noticing work ≠ one organization owning work
```

A company needs durable identity, stable intent, active ownership, hierarchy, authority, attention
and recovery. Those properties must survive the processes currently realizing them.

## Worked example: the checkout defect

A customer reports that checkout accepts an expired address. Three sessions see the support
message:

- one proposes a frontend fix;
- one inspects the API;
- one waits for more detail.

All three may be useful. Nobody can answer: “Who owns returning a verified outcome to the customer
workflow?” If the sessions close, the organization may forget the issue or repeat the same
investigation tomorrow.

Adding a manager model that summarizes their transcripts still does not create durable ownership.
The next step is to turn the external intent into one accepted outcome with a stable identity.

## What changes at this layer?

- Parallelism makes coordination failures visible.
- Routing can improve speed without preventing duplicate or orphaned work.
- A company boundary requires durable organizational state outside any one session.
- Human attention becomes the scarce resource the organization must protect.
