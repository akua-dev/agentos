## The mental model

An autonomous company is built by widening proven boundaries, not by declaring general autonomy on
day one. Choose one domain with observable inputs, bounded consequences and a real definition of
success.

A domain is durable responsibility, not a queue name. Its charter should say:

- what outcomes it owns;
- which projects and external signals belong to it;
- which consequences it can take routinely;
- what returns to First Mate or the Captain;
- how success and failure are observed.

## Worked example: customer-support engineering

The company wants to turn recurring customer defects into reviewed engineering outcomes. Begin with:

> Own technical support defects for the web product. Reconcile accepted support reports with
> existing engineering work, diagnose reproducible software causes and deliver reviewed fixes.
> Escalate customer data access, production mutation, credentials, cross-product scope and merge.

This may justify one persistent Second Mate after the release proves the required lifecycle. It
does not authorize the domain to answer billing disputes, deploy production or browse unrelated
customer data.

Start with one support source and one repository. Watch several complete chains of custody before
adding signals or standing authority.

## What changes at this layer?

- Autonomy gets a measurable organizational boundary.
- A durable charter replaces an open-ended “help the company” prompt.
- Consequences remain explicit while routine execution continues.
- Evidence can justify the next expansion.
